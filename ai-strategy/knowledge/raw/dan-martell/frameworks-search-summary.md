Dan Martell is a SaaS founder/investor and coach for founders/CEOs (SaaS Academy), author of "Buy Back Your Time."

## Core frameworks

**The Buyback Loop** — Audit, Transfer, Fill. Audit your time weekly to find tasks that drain energy, create frustration, or don't require your unique skill set. Transfer those tasks by hiring, systematizing, automating, or documenting so someone else can own them. Fill the freed time with higher-leverage work.

**The Buyback Rate** — Take your income, divide by 2000 (average work hours/year), then divide by 4 (target 4x ROI on delegated time) to get the hourly rate below which you should be buying back your own time rather than doing the task yourself.

**The 4Cs Method (Playbook Creation)** — Start with the area of the business causing the most pain. Record yourself doing the task (e.g. via Loom) at least three times. Transcribe the best recording and refine it with AI into a clear, organized procedure doc with both high-level steps and detailed instructions anyone on the team can follow.

**The 10-80-10 Framework** — First 10%: Ideation, where founder expertise/vision is highest-leverage. Middle 80%: Execution, handed to someone else. Final 10%: Integration, where the founder re-enters to refine, polish, and add their fingerprint.

**The DRIP Matrix / Replacement Ladder** — Manages key hire, ownership, and feeling across 5 levels as a founder progressively delegates: Admin assistant → Delivery → Marketing → Sales → Leadership.

## Recurring stances

- Founder time is the scarcest resource in an early-stage company; the job is to systematically buy it back, not just "work harder."
- Documentation and delegation are treated as an engineering problem (repeatable playbooks), not a soft-skills afterthought.
- Growth in a SaaS business is gated by the founder's ability to get out of execution and into leadership/ideation.
