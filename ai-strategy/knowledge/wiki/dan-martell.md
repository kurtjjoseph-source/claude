## Core ideas

Founder time is the scarcest resource in a growing company — the central discipline isn't "work harder," it's systematically **buying back** your own time so you spend it on the 10% (ideation, leadership) only you can do, not the 80% (execution) anyone else could learn to do.

Delegation is treated as an engineering problem, not a soft-skill: turn any recurring task into a documented, repeatable playbook (record it, transcribe it, refine with AI, hand it off), rather than relying on informal training or hoping people figure it out.

## Vocabulary

- **Buyback Loop** — Audit → Transfer → Fill.
- **Buyback Rate** — (annual income ÷ 2000) ÷ 4 = the hourly rate below which a task should be delegated, not done personally.
- **4Cs Method** — the playbook-creation process (record → transcribe → refine → document).
- **10-80-10** — Ideation / Execution / Integration.
- **DRIP Matrix / Replacement Ladder** — Admin assistant → Delivery → Marketing → Sales → Leadership.

## Stances

- Growth in a SaaS business is gated by how fast the founder can move out of execution and into leadership — capacity, not just capital or market, is often the real bottleneck.
- Systems and documentation compound; ad hoc delegation without a playbook doesn't scale and has to be redone every time someone new is hired.
- A task's value isn't just "does it need doing" but "does it need *me* doing it" — the Buyback Rate makes that a number, not a feeling.

## Recurring stories / framing

- Frames the founder's calendar as the P&L of their own time — the same rigor applied to company financials should apply to how a founder's week is spent.
- Consistently reframes "I don't have time to delegate" as "I haven't built the playbook yet" — the fix is documentation, not more hours.
