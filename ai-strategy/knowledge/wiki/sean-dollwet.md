## Core ideas

1. **First dollar before first system.** Beginners don't need a complex business strategy; they need the confidence loop of a first sale. The steps to $1 online are deliberately NOT the steps to $10K/month — start simple, gain confidence, then add complexity.
2. **The 3-step plan:** (1) Find something people already buy → (2) create a simpler version of it → (3) put it where buyers already hang out. Applied to ebooks on Amazon KDP.
3. **Follow demand, not passion.** Validate every topic against Amazon before writing. Passion in a profitable niche is best case; passion with zero demand is "not a very smart business move."
4. **Validation metric: Best Sellers Rank (BSR).** Look for multiple books under ~80,000 BSR (he checks the *paperback* edition even when selling ebooks, since ebooks convert to paperback/audio later). Tool: free Chrome extension "DS Amazon Quick View" to see BSR on search results.
5. **Simpler ≠ inferior.** A short, hyper-specific book that solves ONE problem for ONE audience ("intermittent fasting for women over 50") is more valuable than a generic everything-book. 10–15K words, written in ~1 day, max 1 week.
6. **Anti-perfectionism.** "You're not writing a masterpiece." Perfectionism = self-sabotage. "Boring is good — it's the boring things that make you money."
7. **AI as assistant, not replacement.** Generate chapter → edit → humanize → next chapter. Never publish 100% AI copy-paste (also a copyright argument: humanized AI content can be copyrighted, raw AI output can't). Expects generic AI (Claude/ChatGPT/Gemini) to degrade/repeat after ~chapter 3 — later chapters need more editing.
8. **Distribution: go where the buyers are.** Don't build a Gumroad/Squarespace/Wix store with zero traffic — list on Amazon KDP (free to join, no fees until a sale, Amazon handles fulfillment/customer service). He's openly dismissive of "build your own store" advice as sponsor-driven.
9. **Books as sellable assets.** A KDP account/book portfolio can be sold like an e-commerce business.
10. **Scaling later:** longer/better books, more books, own marketing (Amazon pushes what already sells), team, ads, paperback/audio/hardcover formats.

## The workflow he demos (prompt-level)

1. Brainstorm: "Give me 50 profitable book topics I can write about" (in Claude).
2. Validate topic on Amazon Books search: page-1 results with BSR < 80,000.
3. "Let's go with the topic of X. Please list out the biggest pain points of this target audience."
4. Pick pain points → "Here are pain points that I think we should focus on solving. Based on this, please create a detailed book outline for this book."
5. "Give me 10 title ideas for this book — versions that are clear, but also catchy." (Clear beats catchy; "Unstuck" is his example of catchy-but-unclear failure.)
6. "Let's go with [title]. Please write the introduction chapter." Then chapter-by-chapter: generate → edit in Google Docs → add personal stories/voice.
7. Pen name or real name — doesn't matter, pick one.
8. Format interior (free tutorial or coauthor.ai tool).
9. Cover: ChatGPT/Gemini image gen (not Claude) or Canva; for self-help, **text-based covers** with the title as the main focus outperform realistic-image covers.
10. Upload to Amazon KDP → live in 1–3 days → check sales in KDP Reports.

## Stances

- **Pro:** Amazon KDP as the most beginner-friendly digital-product model; demand-first topic selection; speed over polish; humanizing AI output; niching down hard.
- **Anti:** building your own store first (Gumroad/Wix/Squarespace/Stan Store), social-media-first traffic strategies, perfectionism, catchy-but-vague titles, publishing raw AI output, chasing passion topics with no demand.
- **Conflicted-interest note:** he sells coauthor.ai and Royalty Hero; the "free way" he teaches is real, but every friction point is also a pitch for his paid tools. Weigh his tool recommendations accordingly.
