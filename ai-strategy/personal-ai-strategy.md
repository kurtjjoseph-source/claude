# Personal AI Implementation Strategy

**Owner:** Kurt Joseph
**Scope:** Personal — across the real threads of your life/work: Vision Outreach Media (client services), the Engage AI product, God's Generation Church ministry, education products, and music
**Starting point:** Not a blank slate — you already have a full activity map (see §2). The open question is *prioritization*: which thread should AI accelerate first, and how much capacity actually exists to do it.
**Time/energy budget:** Nominally 6+ hrs/week, but treat this as best-case, not guaranteed — health (dialysis, WIA) means available time and energy vary week to week. The plan is designed to flex around that rather than assume a fixed block. See [[user-profile-kurt]].
**Horizon:** 8 weeks to a stable, repeatable system; then ongoing maintenance

---

## 1. Why this is a prioritization exercise, not a discovery process

The original framing assumed a blank slate, but your context report makes clear you already have five active threads running in parallel: client/business services (VOM), a specific AI product you're building (Engage AI), an ongoing ministry role with real weekly deliverables (church media/worship/sermon systems), a stack of education products, and music. That's already more than a full plate before "adopt AI more deliberately" is added on top.

So the real question isn't "where could AI help" (everywhere, probably) — it's **which one or two threads get focused AI investment first**, given limited and variable energy. The plan below still has three phases, but Phase 1 is now a *scoring* pass over known threads rather than a from-scratch brainstorm:

1. **Weeks 1–2: Map + Score** — lay out the five threads with real recurring tasks, score each for AI leverage vs. effort vs. energy cost, pick 1–2 to focus on.
2. **Weeks 3–5: Focused Experiments** — build real, repeatable AI workflows in the chosen thread(s).
3. **Weeks 6–8: Consolidation** — lock in what stuck, write down the playbook, decide the steady-state time commitment.

After week 8, this becomes a lightweight quarterly review instead of a weekly project.

## 2. Phase 1 — Map + Score (Weeks 1–2)

### Step 1: The five threads (already known — confirm/update rather than start blank)

| Thread | What it involves | Notes |

| **B. Engage AI product** | Building the church media assistant/AI agent itself — self-service, engagement-focused | Your stated "major current product direction" — likely highest strategic leverage |

### Step 2: Confirm/update this map (do first, ~30–45 min)
Before scoring, quickly check: has anything on this list changed, stalled, or been dropped since the ChatGPT context was captured? Add anything missing (this report may be incomplete or outdated). This replaces a from-scratch activity brainstorm — you're correcting a draft, not writing one.

### Step 3: Score each thread
For each of the five, rate (high/med/low):
- **AI leverage** — how much could AI plausibly speed up or unlock in this thread specifically (e.g. Engage AI is *inherently* an AI project; church announcements are a repetitive drafting task; music teaching is more judgment/presence-heavy)
- **Business/mission value** — revenue, strategic importance, or ministry commitment
- **Energy cost to pursue right now** — factoring in variable health capacity, not just calendar time

### Step 4: Pick 1–2 threads for focused investment
The highest-leverage pick is usually the intersection of high AI-leverage + high value + a task granular enough to finish in a week's worth of variable-energy sessions. **Engage AI** is worth naming explicitly as a likely candidate since it's both your stated top priority *and* literally an AI-native project — but confirm this with Kurt rather than assuming it, since client deadlines (Thread A) or ministry commitments (Thread C) may need to come first in practice.

### Step 5: Run one small experiment in each picked thread this week
Same principle as before — do a real task with AI in the loop rather than researching tools first (e.g., for Engage AI: draft the core user flow or a technical spec with AI; for church ministry: draft this week's MEDEDELINGEN with AI from raw notes; for VOM: draft a client proposal or website copy).

### Step 6: Score each experiment (keep a running log)
For each experiment, note in 2–3 lines:
- Time spent vs. time it would've taken manually
- Quality: was the output usable as-is, needed light editing, or wasn't worth it?
- Would you do this again without being told to?

That last question is the real signal — anything you'd repeat unprompted is a candidate for Phase 2.

## 3. Phase 2 — Focused Experiments (Weeks 3–5)

Take the **1–2 threads picked in Phase 1** and go deeper:

- **Build a repeatable workflow, not a one-off.** E.g., if email drafting scored well, set up a standard prompt/template you reuse, not just a memory of "that one time it worked."
- **Push into slightly harder versions of the task.** If summarizing articles worked, try summarizing a whole meeting or a multi-document research task.
- **Try at least one genuinely new capability**, not just a speed-up — something you couldn't or wouldn't have attempted before (learning a new skill, building a small tool, tackling a project you'd shelved).
- **Deliberately test one failure mode.** Give it a task you suspect it'll do badly (something needing current facts, tacit personal knowledge, or fine judgment) so you calibrate trust levels honestly rather than assuming AI is uniformly good or bad.

Keep the same lightweight log from Phase 1, but now track workflows instead of one-off tasks.

---

## 4. Phase 3 — Consolidation (Weeks 6–8)

- Drop anything that didn't earn a repeat use, even if it seemed promising on paper.
- For the workflows that stuck, write a short **personal playbook** (half a page each): what tool, what prompt/template, what to check before trusting the output.
- Decide on **verification habits per domain** — e.g., always fact-check dates/numbers, always re-read anything sent externally, never let it make a financial/medical/relationship decision unsupervised.
- Set your **ongoing time budget** — likely lower than 6 hrs/week once workflows are built; decide what that steady-state number is.

---

## 5. Weekly time allocation (up to ~6 hrs/week during weeks 1–5, energy-permitting)

Treat this as a ceiling, not a target — on lower-energy weeks, compress proportionally rather than skipping the log/review step, since that's what keeps the plan honest.

| Activity | Time (good week) |
|---|---|
| Running experiments (doing real tasks with AI, within the 1–2 chosen threads) | 3–4 hrs |
| Logging/reviewing results | 30–45 min |
| Learning tool capabilities (new features, prompting techniques) | 1 hr |
| Deliberate "stretch" experiment (something harder/novel) | 1–1.5 hrs |

On a low-energy week: skip straight to whichever single experiment has the best effort-to-value ratio (usually the church ministry recurring task, since it's small and time-boxed), and keep the log entry to one line.

---

## 6. Guardrails (apply from day one, not just at consolidation)

- **Verify before you rely.** Treat AI output on facts, figures, or anything you'll act on as a draft to check, not a finished answer — especially outside domains where you already have expertise to spot errors.
- **Keep judgment calls yours.** Use AI to lay out options and tradeoffs on high-stakes personal decisions; don't outsource the decision itself.
- **Watch for skill atrophy vs. skill growth.** Prefer workflows where AI lets you attempt harder things (growth) over ones where it fully replaces a skill you'd otherwise want to keep sharp.
- **Privacy hygiene.** Be deliberate about what personal/financial/health information you share with which tools.
- **Energy-aware, not calendar-aware.** Plan around variable capacity (health/dialysis) rather than a fixed weekly block — a missed week isn't a failure of the plan, it's the plan working as designed. Adjust the ceiling in §5 down before dropping the practice entirely.

---

## 7. Review cadence after week 8

- **Quarterly:** 30-minute review — what's still in use, what new AI capabilities have emerged worth testing, any workflow due for an upgrade.
- **Ad hoc:** whenever a new model/tool release is notable, do one small test against your existing playbook rather than switching wholesale.

---

## 8. Tracking log

Keep this simple — a running table (in this file or a linked note) with columns: `Date | Activity | Category | Time saved/unlocked | Quality | Repeat? (Y/N) | Notes`. Review it at the end of each phase rather than trying to analyze it in real time.
