01

What VOM actually sells

Three distinct layers of "business" hide behind the same machine. The launcher is not just internal tooling — it is the product.

The product

The launcher is what's sold

VOM sells the done-for-you service itself — "we launch and run a turnkey business for you." The €999 assessment and the membership academy are examples of what it produces, not the ceiling.

VOM's own instance

VOM runs operator-run

VOM builds its own site, payments and leads through the same workstreams — but as the platform root, holding the keys. It can never be a tenant of its own platform.

Every client

Clients run on tenancy

Each client business runs on the tenancy model: Stripe Connect, a delegated DNS zone, phone-only approvals. Sovereign assets, VOM-operated.

02

Where the money goes

VOM never holds the client's money. The bulk of every sale lands in the client's own bank; VOM earns a thin, recurring slice on top.

The structural point: the fee and the retainer both earn only while the business runs on the platform — so revenue is tied to ongoing operation, not to holding the client captive. The platform fee even improves margin as the client grows, at zero extra operator effort.

03

Ownership ≠ attachment

The apparent paradox — "if the client owns everything, won't they just leave?" — dissolves once you separate the two axes. The client is sovereign over the assets and dependent on VOM for the operation. On purpose.

The client owns — outright

Sovereign over every asset

  • Their domain — in their name from day one
  • Their Stripe account & all funds
  • Their content and manuals
  • Their customer & member relationships

No hostage lever — by design.

Attached by value — not lock-in

Four tethers hold them

  • Operational dependency
  • Recurring-revenue model

Retention through service, never captivity.

04

The four tethers

1

Operational dependency

A non-technical owner can't run the hosting, DNS, automations, social swarm, follow-ups or monthly optimization themselves. Leaving means the business stops being operated.

2

Recurring-revenue model

A retainer / membership back-end, not a one-time launch fee. VOM earns by running the business every month — so the relationship is built to continue.

3

Platform fee per transaction

A cut of every sale, for as long as the business runs on the platform. It needs no captivity, and it grows with the client's own success.

4

Genuine switching cost

Tuned automations, the running inventory and benchmark history all live on the platform. Migrating out is real effort — and automated operation is cheaper than hiring a human to replace it.

“We launch and run your business — and it is genuinely yours.”

The pitch is sovereignty, not lock-in. For churches and faith organizations especially, trust beats capture — the client's ownership is the selling point, not a cost to VOM.

05

The one real risk — and its defense

The risk

Clients don't walk because they're trapped — they walk because they stop seeing the value. If VOM operates too invisibly, the owner under-perceives it and considers taking it in-house or hiring cheaper.

The defense — built in

Visible recurring value. The monthly Engage AI report and the per-client running inventory exist to be the monthly proof that justifies the retainer. They are retention infrastructure, not just features.
