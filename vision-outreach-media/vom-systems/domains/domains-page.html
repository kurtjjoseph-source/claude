Questions people actually ask

The honest answers.

Who owns the domain — really?

You do. Your organisation is entered as the registrant, which is the legal owner in the registry's record. We are listed as the administrative and technical contact, which is what lets us do the work. If we disappeared tomorrow, you could contact the registrar directly and take full control.

Why is this cheaper than what we pay now?

We hold a wholesale account with a registrar platform. We pay a small annual membership fee and in exchange get domains at the registry's own price, with no per-domain markup. We pass that price straight to you and absorb the membership fee ourselves. Consumer registrars work the other way round: a very cheap first year, then a renewal price several times the registry cost, every year after.

What if we already have a domain somewhere else?

Keep it. There's no need to move anything. If you'd rather consolidate, we can transfer it in — you request a transfer code from your current provider and we handle the rest. A transfer usually adds a year to the registration at the same at-cost price.

What happens if we stop working with you?

You ask for the transfer code, we send it within two working days, and you move the domain wherever you like. Free. Your site and email keep working throughout — a transfer doesn't interrupt anything. We'll also send your full DNS configuration so whoever takes over can pick it up cleanly.

Can you also set up our email addresses?

Yes. Addresses on your own domain — hello@, office@, giving@ — plus the technical records (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) that decide whether your email is trusted or filtered as spam. Most organisations we meet have never had these configured, which is usually why their newsletters land in spam.

Do we have to buy anything else from you?

No. The domain is not bundled with a contract and has no minimum term. In practice most domains we register are for organisations we're already building something with — but a domain on its own is fine, and it's priced the same either way.
